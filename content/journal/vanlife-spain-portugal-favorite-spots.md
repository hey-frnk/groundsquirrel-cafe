The Iberian Peninsula was the first big vanlife chapter for us and Humbär, our freshly built camper. Below you'll find our favorite spots and the places we parked for the night. And at the very end, the receipts: exactly what one month of vanlife in Spain and Portugal cost us.

## Our route and when we went

![frank and evelyne kissing next to humbär at sunset](/images/journal/vanlife-spain-portugal/vanlife-couple-at-sunset.webp#left)
*Vanlife in Spain and Portugal*

We kicked things off in mid-March in Catalonia, right on the Costa Brava. From there we followed the Mediterranean coast south past Valencia all the way down to Almería, swung west to the Algarve, and then worked our way up Portugal's Atlantic coast through Lisbon and Porto. In May we finally rolled across Galicia, the Picos de Europa and the Basque Country until the road ran out in Bilbao. All in all, we spent about eleven weeks on the road and covered roughly 4,500 kilometers (2,800 miles).

For us, spring was the sweet spot: pleasant temperatures and not a trace of the summer madness yet. In March and April we still crossed paths with plenty of snowbirds who were slowly packing up and heading north. And the big summer wave of vacationers in their camper vans? It hadn't hit yet, not even in May. A lot of places we had practically all to ourselves.

## How we eased into vanlife

![evelyne waving from humbär with all the doors open](/images/journal/vanlife-spain-portugal/welcome-to-vanlife.webp#right)
*Welcome to vanlife!*

Being a vanlifer takes practice, at least it did for us. Same goes for the nerve to park a freshly converted camper on some random roadside, in a country you've never set foot in, and then drift off to sleep without a care in the world.

On day five in the van, we meet Isi. We're puttering along the slow, winding coastal road toward Tossa de Mar when we pull over at a stunning lookout. The spot is on Park4Night, but other people report break-ins there. Our plan is to push on to an official RV area. The place is just too good to leave, though: we film Humbär in golden hour for the very first time, play some music, and that's how we get to know Isi. That encounter is the little nudge we need to start trusting people. We'd figure this out together, and we didn't have to worry nearly as much. That evening, we truly arrived in vanlife.

Riding that new wave of confidence, we head for a sea-view spot above a small village one windy, rainy evening. The road climbs at a 20 percent grade, Humbär's wheels spin, and we claw our way up through the fog by the skin of our teeth. Not ten minutes later, a Swiss van pulls in behind us. And behind the wheel is none other than the person who inspired us to buy a van and hit the road in the first place, on the final days of his own trip and headed home: Chris Jäger.

## Our favorite spots

### Costa Brava: Spain's most spectacular coastline

For us, the Costa Brava is hands down the most spectacular landscape in all of Spain. Rugged cliffs, gnarled pines and tiny coves with water so clear you can count the pebbles on the bottom. The coastal road corkscrews from one bay to the next, and around every bend there's another lookout begging you to pull over. You take it slow in Humbär anyway, and honestly, that's exactly how the Costa Brava wants to be seen. This is where our trip kicked off, and we couldn't have asked for a better start.

### Valencia: a slice of Berlin on the Mediterranean

Valencia gave us serious Prenzlauer Berg vibes. Except here, it's oranges hanging from the trees instead of cherries. Cute little cafés, great restaurants and life spilling out onto the streets. It's the kind of city where you end up staying a few days longer than you planned.

- **Our tip:** the Jardín del Turia. After a massive flood in 1957, the city rerouted its river, and the old riverbed became a park that stretches for about nine kilometers (5.5 miles), perfect for a bike ride or a morning run.
- **Best bakery in Valencia:** [MÒLT Obrador de pa](https://maps.app.goo.gl/5DT3A6Wrr3cL8LXX7)
- **Our favorite neighborhood:** Russafa (peak hipster)

### Cabo de Gata and Europe's only desert

Around Almería, two pretty wild opposites live right next door to each other. Under vast stretches of greenhouses, the so-called "sea of plastic," grows a huge share of the vegetables that land in our supermarkets back home when they're out of season. Just a few miles down the road, the Tabernas Desert begins. It's considered Europe's only true desert, and it has played the backdrop for countless Western movies.

[tip:https://www.alltrails.com/trail/spain/almeria/ruta-circular-de-western-leone|Hike info|Western Leone Loop in the Tabernas Desert, Almería: 8.5 km (5.3 mi) loop, 234 m (768 ft) of elevation gain.|See the trail on AllTrails]

And then there's Cabo de Gata: rugged volcanic landscapes, turquoise water and pale, sun-bleached beaches.

### Seville and Granada: springtime with a Moorish twist

*Wildflowers everywhere: spring in Spain*

![cliffs and ocean at sagres](/images/journal/vanlife-spain-portugal/sagres.webp#right)

### Picos de Europa: our most epic hike

The Ruta del Cares was the most jaw-dropping hike of our entire trip. Roughly 24 kilometers (15 miles) out and back, the trail threads through a deep gorge between sheer rock walls and turquoise water. We just laced up and went for it, and even soft explorers like us made it all the way.

Fun fact: the trail follows a water channel that was carved into the rock in the early 20th century to feed a hydroelectric plant. Locals call the gorge the Garganta Divina, the "divine gorge," and one look tells you why.

## Vanlife in Spain and Portugal

### Parking overnight or wild camping?

Here's the difference that matters: parking overnight means your van is simply parked and you sleep inside. Wild camping means you spread out, with a table, chairs, an awning or the stabilizer legs down.

Wild camping is banned pretty much everywhere in Spain and Portugal, unless it's explicitly allowed, like at some RV areas. So save the camping table and the awning for the campground.

![humbär parked in a green field](/images/journal/vanlife-spain-portugal/life-in-the-van.webp)

![a campfire next to humbär at the campground](/images/journal/vanlife-spain-portugal/campfire-at-the-campground.webp)

*A spot right on the water in Spain*

![evelyne leaning against humbär among the pines](/images/journal/vanlife-spain-portugal/a-spot-in-nature.webp#left)
*A spot out in nature*

Parking overnight is way easier inland than on the coast. Out there, not a soul cares where you park your van. Front row by the sea is a different story. In Galicia we got a visit from the police and watched campers who'd done more than just park get off with a warning. With us, they took a quick look and drove off again, because we didn't have anything set up outside. Apart from the protected areas along the Algarve, we had no trouble finding beautiful spots, even right by the beach.

In Portugal, sleeping in a camper has been regulated by law since 2021: it's banned in protected areas, and outside of them you can stay a maximum of 48 hours in the same spot.

Our most important app was Park4Night. Read the latest comments, especially about break-ins and police checks, and don't just go by the star rating (see: Lagos).

### Infrastructure and supplies

Nowhere in Europe is the camper infrastructure as good as in Spain, and Portugal is top-notch too. Okay, fine, France might have a slight edge. Plenty of towns have RV areas where filling up on fresh water and dumping are free, and in Portugal some supermarkets even have a service point out front.

- **Heads-up for our fellow Swiss:** Switzerland isn't part of EU roaming. We picked up a Spanish 200 GB prepaid SIM from a well-known carrier with a red logo, and had internet and solid coverage the whole way.

### What does vanlife in Spain and Portugal cost?

May was the first month we slept in Humbär every single night. So we were extra curious to find out what vanlife really costs us. That month we drove from Lisbon to Bilbao, spent 26 nights parked out in the wild and 5 nights at campgrounds.

Here's exactly where the money went:

- **Gas:** €350
- **Campgrounds (5 nights):** €101
- **Groceries:** €527
- **Cafés & restaurants:** €295
- **Activities (surfing, hot springs):** €61
- **Transportation (public transit, taxis, rideshares, tolls):** €123
- **Pharmacy:** €12
- **Services:** €33
- **Shopping:** €92
- **Total: €1,594**

That works out to about €51 a day for the two of us, or roughly €800 per person for a whole month on the road.

## Driving on the Iberian Peninsula

### Roads and tolls

![the winding coastal road toward tossa de mar](/images/journal/vanlife-spain-portugal/road-to-tossa-de-mar.webp#right)
*The scenic coastal road to Tossa de Mar*

In Spain, tolls have been scrapped on a lot of highways. We only had to pay on a few stretches up north. Portugal is a different story: outside the Algarve, you'll pay tolls on nearly every highway.

- **Heads-up:** in Portugal, tolls are often collected electronically by overhead gantries, with no booths in sight. If you're driving on foreign plates, register beforehand, for example with EasyToll at the border or with a Toll Card. Otherwise, settling up after the fact turns into a real headache.

We're not big on highways anyway. You just miss too much along the way. The back roads, on the other hand, can get pretty rough. But with Humbär, a little rattle and shake is part of any good road trip.

### Filling up

Humbär runs on gasoline, so it pays to keep an eye on prices: gas was quite a bit cheaper in Spain than in Portugal. Our tip: top off the tank before you cross into Portugal.

## The bottom line

![red cliffs on the algarve at sunset](/images/journal/vanlife-spain-portugal/algarve-sunset.webp#left)
*Sunset on the Algarve*

If there's one thing we're taking home from this trip, it's this: **we had way too little time.**

Eleven weeks sounds like a long road trip. For Spain and Portugal in a slow van, it absolutely isn't. If Humbär had been finished sooner, we would have hit the road back in November or December and spent the whole winter down south.

In southern Spain we'd have loved to explore Gibraltar and the stretch around the Costa del Sol, where other vanlifers rave about winter vanlife. Up north, we'd have spent more time inland and finally given the Pyrenees the proper exploring they deserve.

Spain and Portugal gave us exactly what we'd hoped vanlife would be: nature, amazing food, surfing, hiking, great city trips, and above all the courage to believe that life in a van is something truly, wonderfully special.
