## Humbär's water system

![water running from the tap in our camper](/images/journal/humbaer-water-system/water-from-the-tap.webp#left)
*Water running from the tap*

Our van has a high-quality, modern water setup. Thanks to our filter system we get very good water quality wherever we are, and thanks to a generous fresh water tank we can stay off-grid for several days. The system is comparatively simple, and we were able to put it together ourselves with no previous experience in this field. Here you can read what our water system consists of, how it is built, and what we have learned from living with it.

We built ours into a VW LT35, but nothing about the setup is tied to that van. It is made of parts anyone can buy, and it carries over just as well to any other campervan, panel van or motorhome. What changes from vehicle to vehicle is mainly the size of the tanks and where they can sit.

## Our setup, simple and dependable

![sketch of the water system built into our campervan](/images/journal/humbaer-water-system/the-system-on-paper.webp)
*A sketch of the water system we built in*

![a public water point with two taps, hoses and a cat sitting on the ledge](/images/journal/humbaer-water-system/the-protect-pre-filter.webp#left)
*Filling up at a public water point, under supervision*

The fresh water runs through a tap adapter and our water meter into the water hose. Before it reaches the fresh water tank it passes through a Protect pre-filter. This holds back larger particles such as sand or sediment, and in doing so it also protects the filter stages that follow.

The water then runs through an Active cartridge with activated carbon. Among other things, this reduces chlorine, substances affecting taste and smell, various organic pollutants and other unwanted substances. The filtered water then reaches our fresh water tank, where it is stored.

![a blue alb filter housing on the hose at the van's water inlet](/images/journal/humbaer-water-system/the-filter-at-the-inlet.webp)
*The filter sits on the hose, right before the water goes into the tank*

![a spiral hose running into the fresh water tank, its red lid beside it](/images/journal/humbaer-water-system/the-hose-into-the-tank.webp)
*The filler hose at the fresh water tank, the red inspection lid next to it*

As soon as we need water, we switch on our water pump. It moves the water out of the fresh water tank into our pipework and builds up the pressure needed for the water to flow evenly from the tap. After the pump, the water passes through the Nano germ barrier - a hollow fiber membrane that mechanically holds back germs and bacteria. From there it flows through our mixer tap and out of the faucet.

![our first water pump in the camper](/images/journal/humbaer-water-system/the-first-water-pump.webp)
*Our first water pump (since replaced, see further down)*

![the fresh water tank in the rear right of the vehicle](/images/journal/humbaer-water-system/the-tank-in-the-rear-right.webp)
*Where the fresh water tank sits: rear right in the vehicle*

The used water then drains through the siphon. The siphon forms an odor barrier, because there is always water standing in it. From there the waste water runs into our underfloor waste water tank.

![the drain into the waste water tank under the vehicle](/images/journal/humbaer-water-system/the-drain-under-the-van.webp)
*The drain into the waste water tank under the vehicle*

![demonstrating how the tank empties, using fresh water](/images/journal/humbaer-water-system/emptying-the-waste-tank.webp#right)
*Demonstrating how the tank empties, using fresh water*

Tank electrodes and an LED display let us check the level in both tanks. Once the waste water tank is full, we can empty it through a valve at a suitable gray water disposal station.

For now we decided against a hot water installation. A boiler or an instantaneous water heater could in principle still be retrofitted later. For that, our pipework would have to be extended and the water routed to the heater or to a mixer tap. As things stand, we therefore do not use the hot water side of our mixer tap either.

## Clean, drinkable water, any time

![a drinking water spring in greece](/images/journal/humbaer-water-system/a-spring-in-greece.webp#left)
*A drinking water spring in Greece*

Being as independent and flexible as possible with our drinking water matters a lot to us on the road. We make a point of taking water only from designated drinking water sources. Our filter system then gives us one more stage of filtration before we drink it.

With water from sources like these, we drink it straight from our tap after filtering and do not need to boil it as well.

![clean drinking water thanks to alb filter](/images/journal/humbaer-water-system/clean-water-at-the-tap.webp)
*Clean drinking water thanks to Alb Filter*

We filter our water with high-quality water filters from Alb Filter. Depending on the cartridge used, chlorine, microplastics, pharmaceutical residues, pesticides and PFAS can be reduced or held back. At the same time substances affecting taste and smell can be reduced, which can also improve the taste of tap water.

Another advantage for us: the filter cartridges we use are designed so that valuable minerals stay in the water.

And as a nice side effect, we save a great many single-use bottles on the road and can simply prepare our own water.

## How the filters work

![the pro camper set from alb filter, as delivered](/images/journal/humbaer-water-system/the-pro-camper-set.webp#right)
*The Pro Camper set from Alb Filter, as delivered*

You order the set you need for your project once. In our case that was the Pro Camper set from Alb Filter, for a permanently installed filter system in our vehicle. Alb Filter also offers systems for the home as well as travel filters that do not have to be installed permanently and are easy to take along.

The set contains everything we need for the installation, including a first set of filter cartridges. That also includes the high-quality stainless steel housings the cartridges are fitted into.

After that, all that is needed is to replace the cartridges regularly, depending on use and water quality.

## How long the filters last

How long a cartridge can be used depends among other things on how much water flows through it and on the quality of the water going in. The more particles or other substances the water carries, the harder the filter has to work.

With every new cartridge, Alb Filter includes a small card where we can note the date we put it into service. That way we keep track of when a cartridge went in.

The flow rate can be another sign that a filter should be changed. If the water flow becomes noticeably slower, that can point to a clogged filter.

We also look at the filters themselves regularly. If our pre-filter is heavily soiled, for instance, we swap it out.

As a rough guide we go by the replacement intervals given by the manufacturer. Actual service life can vary with use and water quality.

- **Active cartridge:** approx. 4 months
- **Nano germ barrier:** approx. 6 months
- **Protect pre-filter:** currently approx. 3 months for us

With the pre-filter in particular we notice how much difference water quality makes. Those three months are therefore our own experience, not a generally valid figure.

## Tank cleaning and hygiene

A good filter system alone does not make a hygienically sound water system. The fresh water tank, the pipes, the fittings and the tap all have to be kept clean and washed out regularly.

Precisely because our water stays in the tank for several days, cleaning the system is as much a part of water hygiene for us as changing the filters. We get our cleaning agent from Alb Filter too, and can warmly recommend it.

If you are planning your own water system, we would not only invest in good filters but also build the whole system to be as hygienic and as easy to reach as possible.

If there is one place in your van conversion not to save money, it is here. Especially when you travel a lot, water quality can differ from source to source and from country to country. We would rather not simply trust that water coming out of a tap somewhere automatically has the quality we want for our drinking water.

## Questions and answers

### How big should the water tanks in a camper be?

That depends entirely on how much water you use, what you have planned and how big your camper is.

We built in an 84 l fresh water tank and a 53 l waste water tank. We are very happy with the amount of fresh water. It lets us stay off-grid for up to a week if we use it sparingly.

That said, we like washing dishes with a bit more water, we occasionally use it for showering too, and so we prefer to change our fresh water about every five days.

Our waste water tank sits underfloor. We would have liked it a little bigger - around 70 l would have been ideal for us - but the way our vehicle is built, that unfortunately was not possible.

So it is usually not the fresh water but the waste water that forces us to find a disposal station. With the way we use it, though, our waste water tank also lasts a good four to five days.

### How do you fill a fresh water tank?

![our gardena water hose](/images/journal/humbaer-water-system/the-gardena-hose.webp#left)
*Our Gardena water hose*

For filling up we use our Gardena water hose, which is suitable for drinking water.

At the water source we fit a matching adapter, to which we then connect our water meter and the hose. It pays to carry adapters in several diameters, because connections differ from country to country and from source to source.

We also carry a tap adapter for indoor taps. With it we can fill up even when our adapter will not connect directly to the tap.

At the other end of the hose we fit our Protect pre-filter and the Active cartridge from Alb Filter. That way the water is already pre-filtered while the tank is filling, with various unwanted substances reduced and particles held back before it ever reaches our fresh water tank.

### How does the water get from the tank to the tap?

![our water pumps: the old one on the left, the new one on the right](/images/journal/humbaer-water-system/the-old-and-new-pumps.webp#right)
*Our water pumps: the old one on the left, the new one on the right*

We built in a pressure water pump, which we switch on with an on/off switch.

The pump moves the water out of the fresh water tank into our pipework and builds up the pressure needed. That is what lets the water flow evenly from our tap.

After the pump, the water passes through the Nano germ barrier. The hollow fiber membrane inside it mechanically holds back germs and bacteria.

From there the water goes through our mixer tap and out of the faucet.

### How do we shower in vanlife?

Because our vehicle only offers limited space, we decided against a shower inside.

Instead we carry a simple solar shower that we can hang on the outside of the van. When it is colder, we heat the water in our kettle and then pour it into the solar shower.

When showering outdoors we make sure to use biodegradable products and to keep a good distance from any body of water.

We also quite like visiting a public shower now and then, going to an indoor pool, or using our emergency shower - for which we simply turn our big kitchen sink into a small "shower".

### How did we manage to fit an ordinary household tap in the camper?

The installation is fairly simple in principle. Hardware stores sell adapter pieces that let you connect an ordinary household tap to a camper's pipework.

With the pump, though, you do have to make sure it suits the pressure required and the tap you are using. Which connectors and adapters you need depends on the tap itself and on the pipework you have.
